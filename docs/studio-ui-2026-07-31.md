# The studio sorted 39 views by what the model calls them, not by what an analyst asks

A UI and UX study of Power Dispatch Studio. It rebuilds the shell and ships it.
Evidence: 40 screenshots of the old shell in `tmp/uiux-20260731/views/`, 25 of the
new one in `tmp/uiux-20260731/after/`. Every claim below comes from one of them.

## What the tool answers, and who holds it

A WESM market analyst has four questions and a deadline. Can the grid host the
announced data-center wave. Where would those megawatts have to sit. What that
does to the spot price and to the Meralco bill. And, before any of it counts,
whether the model is right.

The studio can answer all four. It holds 39 destinations: 10 solution views,
19 analyses, 2 planning phases, 5 input tables, and the scenario tools. Nothing
below is about the analysis, which is strong. The Siting view names its finding
in the heading, labels its chart directly, and glosses its own legend. The
Backcast prints MAE, bias, correlation and hit rate for all three grids. Those
views did not need work.

The shell around them did.

## The old shell spent 190 of a 900-pixel laptop before a number appeared

Three navigation systems stacked on top of each other, for one application.

A ribbon with three tabs (Home, Model, Solution) carried six live controls
between them. Two of the six were static text and one was a permanently disabled
button. An explorer with two more tabs (System, Simulation) listed 39 items
flat, in five groups. A breadcrumb under both repeated where you were.

Measured on the screenshots: title bar 54px, ribbon 84px, explorer tabs and
breadcrumb 52px. On a 1440x900 laptop that is 190 pixels, 21 percent of the
window, before the first figure.

At 390px it broke. `studio-quick-phone.png` shows "Close studio" clipped by the
viewport edge. The explorer collapsed to a horizontal strip showing only its
tail items, Regions, Storage and Quick scenario, with no sign that 36 others
existed above them. That is not a missing media query. Three stacked navigation
systems cannot fold into one phone screen.

At 1920 it wasted the other end. `studio-quick-desk.png` stops at about 1560px
and leaves the right third empty.

## The groups were named after the model, so the question had to be translated

The explorer's five groups were Objects, Solution, Analysis, Phases, Shortcut.
Under Objects: Generators, Fuels, Interfaces, Regions, Storage. Under Solution:
Merit order, Window band, Coupled flows.

An analyst who wants to know what a WESM move does to a household bill has to
already know that it sits under Analysis, and that it is called Bill impact.
There was no search over the 39. The map at `/` has a plant finder; the studio
had nothing.

Four more consequences fell out of the same cause:

- **The region control appeared and vanished.** Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao
  showed only on the views that read one grid. On the others the control was
  simply absent, so nothing said whether Luzon still applied.
- **No view was addressable.** Only a shared scenario carried a URL (`#m=`).
  You could not send a colleague the Visayas backcast; you had to tell them
  which item to click.
- **The answer left the screen on every move.** The solved prices lived inside
  whichever view you had open. Switching lens meant losing the result.
- **Three places reported one state.** A Solved chip in the title bar, a Run
  button that greyed out, and a footer repeating the scenario and edit count.

## The rebuild: the shell asks the question, the dock keeps the answer

`studio/src/shell/nav.ts` holds the whole information architecture. Every one of
the 39 destinations keeps its old `Nav` value, so no view component changed.
What changed is how you reach them.

**Eight groups, named as the analyst's questions.** Tonight's market (6), Is
there headroom (6), Where it can sit (4), Prices and bills (6), The build-out
(4), My scenarios (5), Is the model right (3), Model inputs (5). The group
holding the open view expands; the rest stay collapsed. Every item carries a
one-line hint saying what it answers.

**A command palette over all 39.** Cmd+K, Ctrl+K, or a lone slash opens it. It
matches the label, the hint, and a keyword list, so "meralco" finds Bill impact
and "lmp" finds Nodal prices. Arrow keys move, Enter opens.

**Every view is a URL.** `#v=backcast&g=visayas` opens the Visayas backcast.
The slug rides beside the existing `#m=` scenario payload, so a shared scenario
and a chosen view survive the same link. This uncovered a real defect during the
test pass: `App.tsx` only opened the studio for `#m=`, so a `#v=` link landed
on the hero. The same pass fixed it.

**One chrome bar, 52 pixels, holding every scenario control.** Search, region,
scenario, new scenario, Run, theme, close. The region buttons no longer vanish
on the views that read all three grids; they disable, and the tooltip says why.
The Run button carries the solve state in its own label and colour, so the
duplicate chip is gone.

**The run dock is the piece that makes it a what-if tool.** A 288px column on
the right, pinned across all 39 views, shows each grid's clearing price,
reserve margin and loss-of-load probability, with the move against a
base-case solve that runs once on mount. Below 980px it becomes a horizontal
strip under the bar rather than disappearing, because losing the answer is the
defect it exists to fix.

Building it surfaced something the old UI hid. The Quick scenario levers preview
against the calibrated base; they do not write the model. Move the data-center
slider to 2,400 MW and the panel prints ₱12.00 while the solved model is still
at ₱6.00, and nothing on the old screen said so. The dock now shows a separate
amber band, "Lever preview, not yet in the model", above the solved cards. No
card ever mixes a previewed number with a solved one.

## Four breakpoints, each dropping the least useful column

| Width | Layout |
|---|---|
| 1440 and up | rail 244, canvas, dock 288. At 1920 that fills the window exactly |
| 1181 to 1439 | rail 220, canvas, dock 262 |
| 981 to 1180 | dock collapses to a 40px spine by default, rail stays |
| 721 to 980 | rail becomes a drawer with a scrim, dock moves above the view as a strip |
| 720 and under | the bar wraps to three rows, the wordmark drops, nothing clips |

A screenshot at 1920, 1440, 1100, 900 and 390, in light and dark, backs each
row. At 900 and 1100 a scripted check confirms the bar no longer overflows.

## Kept, on purpose

The colour tokens, the Fira Sans and Fira Code pairing, and the data-series
palette did not change. The `ui-ux-pro-max` design-system query for a data-dense
energy analyst dashboard returns the values already in `tokens.css`, and the
series colours are shared with the map so a fuel reads the same in both places.
One token group is new: a chrome layer that stays deep in both themes, so the
furniture recedes and the data floats in front of it.

## What the gates cover, and what they do not

Green: TypeScript, oxlint, 114 vitest tests, `make qa` (data pins, LP parity,
nodal, sites, perspective, engine sync, banned framings), `verify_claims` at
98 of 98, and the map e2e at 35 pass 0 fail. Two demo recorders drove the
old explorer by class name; both now drive the new rail.

## The views were not the problem I said they were

The first draft of this study ended by saying the 39 view bodies scrolled
sideways below 900px and needed a per-view pass. Then I measured all 39 at
1280, 1024, 900 and 390. Exactly one view overflowed its canvas, Bill impact,
and only by 12 pixels on a phone. The claim was wrong.

The real fault was shared and different. `.stat-row` floored its grid tracks at
150px, so a 366px canvas packed two tiles at 160px and a 760px canvas packed
four at 177px. A label like "Evening residual, 2026-06-10" then wrapped to three
lines above its value. 29 of the 39 views hit it. The floor is now
`min(100%, 210px)`: one tile on a phone, three at 1024, and the `min()` guard
stops a track ever outgrowing a narrow canvas.

Bill impact overflowed because its `.scn` grid stacked to `1fr` under 760px, and
a grid item keeps `min-width: auto`, so a wide child still pushed the row out.
`minmax(0, 1fr)` is what actually stacks.

Wide tables were the other half. 18 of them scroll sideways on a phone, which is
correct for a data table, but they cut at the viewport edge with no mark, so the
table read as ending there. `ui/ScrollBox.tsx` measures which side still holds
columns and writes `data-more`; the stylesheet fades that edge. It uses a mask
rather than a background gradient, because row backgrounds paint over a
background and a slate shadow is invisible on the dark theme. A table that fits
carries no fade, and the last column is never dimmed once the reader reaches it.

Measured after the change: zero canvas overflow and zero tiles under 200px, at
all four widths.

## The limit, and why it is here

18 tables still scroll sideways on a 390px phone. They now say so, but saying so
is not the same as fitting. A real phone treatment would drop each row to a card,
and that is per-view work across 18 files rather than one shared rule.

Two of the four faults above were found only by measuring, and the fault I
predicted in prose did not exist. That is the part worth carrying forward: a
layout claim about 39 views is a measurement, not a reading.
