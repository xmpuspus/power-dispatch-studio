Pax Silica, New Clark City, Capas, Tarlac

Pax Silica would draw more power than the whole Visayas grid, and use up to twice as much water as everyone in Makati. Every day.

In July 2026 the Bases Conversion and Development Authority, the government agency developing Pax Silica, put numbers on its Pax Silica campus: 3,000 MW of power at full development, and 65 to 90 million liters of water a day. Numbers that size mean nothing on their own. This page draws each one against something you already know, and then follows what it would actually take to supply.

Announced figures are labeled with their owner. Everything else is arithmetic you can check, or a result from this project's grid model, whose own sources sit on every chart below.

Pax Silica's 3,000 MW is more than the entire Visayas grid asked for at its highest recorded moment

Start with the number itself. BCDA's figure for Pax Silica at full development is 3,000 MW ("at full development, mga three gigawatts po iyan" was the exact quote), held around the clock, because chip plants and data centers do not sleep. Put it beside the three island grids on one scale. NGCP's own June 2026 bulletins agree on the Visayas number: evening peaks of 2,384 to 2,482 MW against 2,581 to 2,691 MW available.

So scale the answer up. To average 3,000 MW across a cloudless model day you need at least 12,200 MW of panels. On the land density of a real Philippine build, MTerra Solar's 3,500 MW on 3,500 hectares one province over, that is 122 square kilometers of panels. Now draw that on places you know.

Covering Pax Silica's 3,000 MW around the clock from sunlight alone would take about 12,200 MW of panels, which is 122 km² at the land a real Philippine solar farm uses (MTerra Solar, 1 hectare per megawatt). The square is that area at true scale over Metro Manila, the Philippine capital region. The panels would not go here: Pax Silica is about 95 km north, and the map is only a place to see the size against. Makati, marked, fits inside the square. Clouds only make it bigger.

For scale, all of New Clark City covers 94.5 km² and MTerra Solar, the biggest solar-and-battery build anywhere, covers 35 km². The 11 dark hours would also need about 33,000 megawatt-hours of batteries, 7 times the 4,500 megawatt-hours MTerra Solar is building.

The two power lines into Pax Silica can carry 770 of the 3,000 MW it needs

The second answer is the grid: generate elsewhere, deliver over wires. Pax Silica connects to the rest of Luzon through two 230 kilovolt (kV) circuits of one Concepcion to Clark route. Solving one recorded day on the public grid map, the most Pax Silica could draw through them at the 7pm peak is about 770 MW, and one of the two circuits is its only route in. Lose that one and the map shows no path at all.

Ask the grid to serve Pax Silica anyway, and the modeled price of Luzon electricity doubles, all day

Suppose the wires were not the problem. Serving 3,000 MW more means running plants that cost more, and the marginal plant is what sets the price for everyone, in pesos per kilowatt-hour (kWh). This project's cost model replays the same recorded day, 25 June 2026, with and without Pax Silica. Without it, coal sets the Luzon price in all 24 hours, at the coal cost the Energy Regulatory Commission administers, P6.00 per kWh (the administered price IEMOP publishes). With it, oil is the marginal plant in all 24 hours and the price is P12.00. Not a spike at the evening peak. The whole day, doubled, for every buyer on the grid.

Proposed Pax Silica data-center and chip campus, New Clark City, Philippines
Serving Pax Silica from the grid would double the modeled price of Luzon electricity
Every power plant on Luzon, the Philippines' biggest grid, lined up cheapest to priciest, on one recorded evening, 25 June 2026. A block's width is its megawatts and its height is its cost in Philippine pesos per kilowatt-hour. The last plant needed sets the price everyone pays, so each line stops inside that plant rather than at its edge: only part of it runs. The same fuel appears twice at two costs, because two plants burning it are not equally cheap.

If that cost reached households, P6.00 more per kilowatt-hour is P1,200 a month more on a 200 kWh bill (a model figure, not a forecast). The cables to the other Philippine grids also fill up, and the price gap across a full cable is money someone pays: about P38 million in this one modeled day, with the Visayas evening price pulled up to P11.98.

A what-if on the cost model, not a forecast. It holds Pax Silica at a flat 3,000 MW and ignores delivery. Only about 770 MW can physically reach Pax Silica over its two existing power lines, and real evening prices already run above this stack. Coal at P6.00 is the price administered by IEMOP, the Independent Electricity Market Operator of the Philippines, and gas is a freedom-of-information answer, but the P12.00 oil price is a labelled assumption, a peaker proxy in the fuel-cost table, where it is labelled an assumption.

The market as it runs now prices congestion small and sparse. The published nodal congestion component of the Wholesale Electricity Spot Market (WESM) fires on 28 of 70 sampled days, a median of P0.56 per kWh, at most P19 on 26 May 2026 (IEMOP's nodal price files).

Pax Silica's own power station is the actual plan, and it still leans on the same two power lines

Which is why BCDA says Pax Silica will build its own power rather than take 3,000 MW from the grid. Follow that plan at 7pm: say it builds 2,500 MW of its own and draws the last 500 over the lines. The bar fills. It works.

Then one 600 MW unit trips, a generator about the size of one of the two units at the Sual coal plant in Pangasinan, the most ordinary event in a power system. The lines are already asked for everything they carry, and 331 MW of Pax Silica has no source. Building your own station does not remove the grid, it turns the grid into your backup, and the backup comes down the same two circuits.

At 65 to 90 million liters a day, Pax Silica would use up to twice as much water as everyone living in Makati

Power is only half the announcement. Water arrives with Pax Silica no matter who generates its electricity: cooling for the data centers, ultrapure water for the chip plants. BCDA's own projection is 65 to 90 million liters a day. Take BCDA at their word and the division is simple. Here is that number three ways.

Each square is one liter in a hundred. The two rust squares are Pax Silica's share and the other 98 are the rest of the 4,000 million liters a day Metro Manila draws from the dam. Pax Silica's exact share is 1.6 to 2.2 percent.

The small print, exactly: Pax Silica's announced 65 to 90 million liters a day is the tap water of 433,000 to 600,000 people at Metro Manila's 150 liters per person a day; Makati counts 309,770 people (2024 census), about 46 million liters; the pond is sized at 120 million liters a day; the Angat share is 1.6 to 2.2 percent; an Olympic pool holds 2.5 million liters; and as irrigation the same water would keep 520 to 900 hectares of rice flooded for a 120-day season.

Both sides of the argument are on the record. The National Water Resources Board says local sources can supply Pax Silica "without adversely affecting the local water requirement." Farmer and environmental groups, and Aeta communities on the land, dispute it; coverage puts potentially affected residents near 20,000 while BCDA counters that only about 10 farmers are directly affected. No independent water balance for Pax Silica has been published. That absence is the finding.

The designated site is 1,620 hectares of what reporting calls rice and coconut land, and no tree count for it has been published

Power and water are what Pax Silica would consume. The land is what it would occupy. BCDA president Joshua Bingcang put the designated industrial area at 1,620 hectares, and the Manila Bulletin describes the same footprint as a 4,000-acre economic security zone where "much of the land under discussion supports rice, coconut, and other food production". Hectares are as hard to picture as megawatts, so draw them against the city they sit in and two places whose size people know.

Proposed Pax Silica data-center and chip campus, New Clark City, Philippines
The site is 1,620 hectares, about nine tenths of Makati's land area
One square is 50 hectares. The first grid is all of New Clark City, 9,450 hectares, with the 1,620 designated for Pax Silica marked in rust, which is 17.1 percent of the city. The runs below use squares of the same size, so their lengths can be compared directly, to the nearest square. Rust is Pax Silica, hollow rust is land already leased to it, navy is a place its size is measured against, and pale is the rest of New Clark City. Makati is a city in Metro Manila, and Bonifacio Global City is a business district in Taguig, the city next to it. The Manila Bulletin describes the land as rice, coconut and other food production. How many trees stand on it is not public, because no inventory has been published.

The tree count does not exist. No environmental compliance certificate has been issued, the Department of Environment and Natural Resources says it is still assessing the project, and no tree inventory or tree-cutting permit has been published. Any number of trees felled would be invented.
The two counts of people do not cover the same ground. BCDA, the Bases Conversion and Development Authority building Pax Silica, says about 10 farmers are directly affected by Pax Silica's 1,620 hectares. Kalikasan, an environmental network, puts displacement from the whole New Clark City project at 20,000 Indigenous people and 15,000 farmers. One counts Pax Silica's own hectares, the other the whole city around it, so the two cannot be subtracted.
For the wider setting, 30 percent of Capas, the Tarlac town holding Pax Silica, is productive agricultural land, on the municipal government's own data.

What this page cannot know. The grid operator NGCP, the National Grid Corporation of the Philippines, does not publish what the two circuits are rated to carry, so 770 rests on the standard rating for 230 kV lines and moves if the real rating differs. The grid model snaps Pax Silica to a bus 8.6 km away, on community-mapped routes, not the connection anyone will build.
