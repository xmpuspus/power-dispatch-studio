Pax Silica in New Clark City, Capas, Tarlac

Pax Silica needs 3,000 MW and 65 to 90 million liters of water daily

The Bases Conversion and Development Authority announced both full-development figures in July 2026. Construction starts in 2028, with full development 10 to 15 years later.

The charts compare those figures with public market, grid, water, and land records. They label every calculation and assumption.

Pax Silica's 3,000 MW exceeds the highest recorded Visayas demand

The comparison uses 117 archived days. Luzon peaked at 14,232 MW. Visayas peaked at 2,744 MW and Mindanao at 2,748 MW.

Announced Pax Silica demand compared with recorded island-grid demand
Pax Silica would use more power than the entire Visayas grid
Block area shows megawatts (MW). Pax Silica is announced future demand. The island blocks show recorded five-minute demand. Island populations, 2020 census, give a second size reference.

Visayas forecasts around the same period ranged from 2,384 to 2,482 MW against 2,581 to 2,587 MW available. These are day-ahead values.

The market operator supplies the grid records. The Bases Conversion and Development Authority supplies the 3,000 MW figure.

The cost model moves Luzon from P6.00 to P12.00 per kWh

The calculation removes transmission delivery as a constraint and adds the full 3,000 MW to Luzon. It estimates one modeled day under stated assumptions.

A flat-block cost calculation for Luzon
Serving Pax Silica from the grid would double the modeled price of Luzon electricity
The base case averages P6.00 per kWh. Here kWh means kilowatt-hour. Added demand averages P12.00. Oil is the last block dispatched in 16 of those hours. The calculation leaves 664 MW of the supply stack unused. MW means megawatts.

The Visayas peak rises to P11.98. Link congestion rent is about P38 million in this one modeled day. Full household pass-through would add P1,200 a month to the 200 kWh example bill.

Resolution No. 10, Series of 2026 supplies the P6.00 value. The model labels the P12.00 oil value as an assumption. The nodal record shows congestion on 28 of 70 sampled days. Its nonzero median is P0.56 per kWh and its highest sampled value is P19.28 on 26 May 2026.

Sources. Coal price rule · Nodal prices

Pax Silica's water figure exceeds Makati household planning demand

BCDA states 65 to 90 million liters a day. The comparison uses a household planning value and does not replace a local water study.

Announced daily water demand compared with public planning figures
The water Pax Silica asked for is 1.4 to 1.9 times the daily water planned for everyone in Makati
The Makati calculation uses 309,770 residents and 150 liters per resident. It equals planning demand for 433,000 to 600,000 people. It excludes offices and industry.
Pax Silica water demand
Proposed harvesting facility
Comparison with the Angat allocation

The range equals irrigation for 500 to 690 hectares at 1.5 liters per second per hectare. It equals 1.6 to 2.3 percent of the Angat allocation. For scale, an Olympic pool holds 2.5 million liters. Pax Silica would use local water from the Sacobia watershed.

The National Water Resources Board says local sources are enough. The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) supplied the project figure. Community and environmental groups dispute the water board's claim.

The 1,620-hectare project area equals 17.1% of New Clark City

The public record gives an area and competing accounts of land use. It gives no tree inventory for Pax Silica.

Announced project area compared with familiar places
The site is 1,620 hectares, about nine tenths of Makati's land area
Each square equals 50 hectares. Pax Silica covers 17.1 percent of the city by area, or 32 of the 189 squares shown for New Clark City.

No public tree inventory was found for Pax Silica. The Bases Conversion and Development Authority (BCDA) issued the compliance certificate for the project masterplan. Each company locating inside Pax Silica must still get its own.

BCDA reports about 10 farmers directly affected. Its affected-person list names O'Donnell, Aranguren, and Santa Lucia. Advocacy reporting cites 20,000 Indigenous people and 15,000 farmers for wider New Clark City. These counts cannot be subtracted. Public records say over 30 percent of Capas is productive farmland. BCDA projects 130,000 to 190,000 direct jobs.

Sources. BCDA area · Land concerns
